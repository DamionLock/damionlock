Most AI infrastructure thesis debates start with the wrong question. 'On-prem or frontier?' is a procurement argument dressed up as strategy. I think on-prem should be the default for anything that touches your crown-jewel data, and frontier models should earn their place case by case. I might be wrong, but here is how I got there.

## Why does the AI infrastructure thesis keep going wrong?

Because people pick a side before they know the workload.

Over 36 years in infrastructure and 500+ projects delivered, across 7+ industries, I have watched the same pattern: the loudest option wins the first meeting, and the data wins the last one, usually too late.

Frontier APIs are brilliant for speed. Owning the stack is brilliant for control. Neither is a personality.

## What does sovereign actually mean?

Not a flag on a brochure. It means you can answer three things without phoning a vendor: where the data sits, who can read it, and what happens if the service changes its terms or disappears.

Data is liquid gold, not oil. You do not pour it into someone else's refinery and hope for the best.

## Where does frontier still win?

I will say the contrarian bit plainly: plenty of teams over-build. If your task is general drafting, public-data summarisation or a two-week prototype, renting a frontier model is the sensible, lower-carbon choice. Buying racks for that is vanity.

On-prem wins when the data is sensitive, the volume is steady, latency matters, or the regulator asks awkward questions. Industry analysts have noted that cloud bills for steady, high-utilisation workloads can overtake owned-infrastructure costs within a few years (a16z, 'The Cost of Cloud, a Trillion Dollar Paradox', 2021).

## How do I decide? A four-step test

- **Classify the data.** What would hurt if it leaked or was retrained on?
- **Profile the load.** Spiky favours renting. Steady favours owning.
- **Price the exit.** What does leaving cost in months and money?
- **Build for being wrong.** Pick modular kit you can move, resize or retire.

That last step matters most. I once sat through roughly 45 data-centre designs where the brave ones were the ones that planned for the forecast to be wrong. Mobile and modular beats the mega-build because you can change your mind without a demolition crew.

## What about water and power?

Owning compute means owning the footprint. Design for water-smart cooling from day one, not as a retrofit. Responsible by default is simply the cheaper engineering choice over a decade.

Takeaway: sovereignty is a decision about data, not a decision about buildings. Make that call first, and the hardware gets easier.
